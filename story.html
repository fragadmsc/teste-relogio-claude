The Meridian Diver

Made to outlive every moment it measures.

Every Fraga timepiece is assembled by hand over eleven days. Sapphire crystal, a self-winding movement and a case polished until it holds light like water.

I · 1987 · Ubatuba

A tide table and a broken clock.

Tomás Fraga grew up in a fishing village on the coast of São Paulo, where his father repaired boat engines and his grandmother kept a brass wall clock that never told the right time. At eleven, he took it apart on the kitchen table. It took him a whole winter to understand why it ran slow: salt air had crept into the gears.

II · 2006 · Lausanne

Learning to let go of speed.

After a scholarship to a watchmaking school in Switzerland, Tomás spent nine years as a bench watchmaker for a small independent atelier. He learned patience, tolerances of a few microns, and one rule he never forgot: a watch is finished only when nobody can tell how hard it was to make.

III · 2015 · The Workshop

Back to the sea.

Tomás returned home and opened a two-room workshop in a converted net-mending shed, a hundred steps from the water. The first Fraga watch, a steel diver with a midnight-blue dial inspired by the sea after sunset, took him fourteen months to build. He made six, and sold them to friends who had waited years for him to come back.

IV · 2019 · The Storm

The night everything stopped.

A winter storm flooded the workshop and ruined most of the tooling and half of a year's production. Tomás and his small team of four worked through the night to save the movements, drying every part by hand. Out of that night came the brand's promise: every Fraga is built to survive salt, water and time, and is tested in the sea before it ships.

V · Today

Eleven days, one watch.

Today the workshop has twelve watchmakers and still builds fewer than four hundred watches a year, each assembled by a single pair of hands over eleven days and signed on the movement. Tomás still closes the workshop himself, and walks to the water before going home.
